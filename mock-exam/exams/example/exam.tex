{\Large\bfseries Introduction to Game Theory: Quiz 1}\hfill 30 minutes, 25 points

Closed book. One handwritten A4 sheet (both sides) is allowed. No calculator.
Justify all answers.

\bigskip
\textbf{Question 1 (6 points).} Consider the following game, where player 1 chooses a row and player 2 chooses a column.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c|ccc}
  & $L$ & $C$ & $R$ \\ \hline
$U$ & \cell{3}{2} & \cell{1}{3} & \cell{0}{1} \\
$M$ & \cell{2}{2} & \cell{2}{2} & \cell{1}{1} \\
$D$ & \cell{1}{3} & \cell{1}{1} & \cell{4}{0} \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Eliminate dominated strategies iteratively. Which strategies survive? Show each elimination step.

\bigskip
\textbf{Question 2 (12 points).} Consider the game

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c|cc}
  & $L$ & $R$ \\ \hline
$T$ & \cell{4}{2} & \cell{0}{0} \\
$B$ & \cell{0}{0} & \cell{2}{4} \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(a)] (4 points) Find all pure-strategy Nash equilibria.
  \item[(b)] (6 points) Find the mixed-strategy Nash equilibrium in which both players randomize.
  \item[(c)] (2 points) Compute player 1's equilibrium payoff.
\end{enumerate}

\bigskip
\textbf{Question 3 (7 points).} Suppose that in a finite strategic game every player $i$ has a strictly dominant strategy $s_i^*$.
Prove that $s^* = (s_1^*, \dots, s_n^*)$ is a Nash equilibrium and that it is the only Nash equilibrium in pure strategies.
