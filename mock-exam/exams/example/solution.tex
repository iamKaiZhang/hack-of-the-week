{\Large\bfseries Quiz 1: sample solution and rubric}

\textbf{Question 1 (6 points).} Intended reading: iterated elimination of \emph{strictly} dominated strategies (IESDS), as defined in the lecture.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Step 1: $R$ is strictly dominated by $C$ for player 2 (payoffs $3>1$, $2>1$, $1>0$). Remove $R$. \hfill (2 points)
  \item Step 2: in the reduced game, $D$ is strictly dominated by $M$ for player 1 ($2>1$ against $L$, $2>1$ against $C$). Remove $D$. \hfill (2 points)
  \item Step 3: nothing else is strictly dominated ($U$ vs $M$: $3>2$ but $1<2$; $L$ vs $C$: $2<3$ but $2=2$). Surviving strategies: $\{U, M\}$ for player 1, $\{L, C\}$ for player 2. \hfill (2 points)
\end{itemize}
Rubric notes: if a student eliminates weakly dominated strategies ($L$ weakly dominated by $C$ after step 2, leaving $(M, C)$), award at most 4 points unless the student explicitly states they use weak dominance, in which case award 5.

\textbf{Question 2 (12 points).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item (a) $(T, L)$ and $(B, R)$. 2 points each; $-1$ for each extra (wrong) profile listed.
  \item (b) Let player 1 play $T$ with probability $p$ and player 2 play $L$ with probability $q$.
  Player 2 indifferent: $2p = 4(1-p) \Rightarrow p = 2/3$. Player 1 indifferent: $4q = 2(1-q) \Rightarrow q = 1/3$.
  Equilibrium: player 1 plays $(2/3, 1/3)$, player 2 plays $(1/3, 2/3)$.
  3 points for each probability with correct indifference condition; 1 point each if the probabilities are swapped (a common mistake).
  \item (c) Intended: the mixed equilibrium from (b). Player 1's payoff is $4q = 4/3$. 2 points.
  Rubric notes: award full credit for payoffs 4 at $(T,L)$ and 2 at $(B,R)$ if clearly labeled by equilibrium.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Question 3 (7 points).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Nash equilibrium (3 points): for every $i$, strict dominance gives $u_i(s_i^*, s_{-i}) > u_i(s_i, s_{-i})$ for all $s_i \neq s_i^*$ and all $s_{-i}$, in particular for $s_{-i} = s_{-i}^*$. So no player gains by deviating from $s^*$.
  \item Uniqueness (4 points): let $s'$ be a pure Nash equilibrium with $s'_i \neq s_i^*$ for some $i$. Strict dominance gives $u_i(s_i^*, s'_{-i}) > u_i(s'_i, s'_{-i})$, a profitable deviation, contradicting that $s'$ is an equilibrium.
  \item Deduct 2 points if the argument only compares against $s_{-i}^*$ in the uniqueness part (circular). Deduct 1 point for missing ``for all $s_{-i}$''.
\end{itemize}
